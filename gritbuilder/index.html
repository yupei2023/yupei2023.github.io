Design context

Grit becomes useful when it is connected to daily behavior.

Long-term competencies—academic communication, writing, music, athletics, and professional skills—develop through repeated effort. Yet practice opportunities are often scattered, slow progress is easy to underestimate, and time spent can be mistaken for meaningful improvement.

Meaningful practice

Track the conditions for improvement, not only the clock.

A session becomes meaningful when it shows evidence of deliberate engagement. GritBuilder asks learners to notice five features:

  • 01
    Clear goal

    I knew what I was trying to improve.

  • 02
    Focused effort

    I was actively engaged, not on autopilot.

  • 03
    Appropriate challenge

    The practice stretched me without becoming overwhelming.

  • 04
    Feedback

    I received information from a person, recording, or result.

  • 05
    Reflection

    I identified what changed and what to try next.

Who it is for

Designed around sustained learning in everyday life.

Primary users

Adult learners

Graduate students, second-language learners, musicians, athletes, hobbyists, and professionals developing a long-term competency through repeated practice.

Secondary users

Parents, mentors, and coaches

People who support another learner’s practice and want a lightweight structure for discussing effort, feedback, challenge, and next steps.

Research status

A prototype and research instrument—not a finished product.

GritBuilder was created for a Human–Computer Interaction Research & Analysis mini project. Its design draws on information-processing theory, cognitive-load theory, deliberate practice, and grit-related behavior.

Current stage

Working interactive prototype

The current version supports goals, customized practice categories, session logging, reflection, progress views, local export, and reset.

Planned evaluation

Iterative HCI research

Proposed next steps include cognitive walkthroughs, usability testing with 5–8 target users, and a short diary study of real practice.

Interpretation boundary

Behavior, not a grit score

The prototype supports reflection on practice routines. It does not diagnose grit, predict success, or evaluate a learner’s character.
