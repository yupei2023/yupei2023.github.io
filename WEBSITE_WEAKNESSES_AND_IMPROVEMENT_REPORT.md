# Ph.D. Committee Review: Website Weaknesses and Required Improvements

**Website reviewed:** <https://yupei2023.github.io/>  
**Primary focus:** Ph.D. Portfolio  
**Review date:** July 30, 2026  
**Review perspective:** Program of Study committee member  

## Scope and Review Standard

This report intentionally records only weaknesses, risks, omissions, inconsistencies, and recommended corrections. It evaluates whether the website communicates doctoral development through credible evidence, clear language, coherent organization, and accessible presentation.

The review used the published website source in the GitHub repository. The central standard applied to the Portfolio was whether a committee member could identify:

1. The competencies being claimed
2. The evidence supporting each claim
3. Yupei Duan’s individual contribution
4. The learning or development demonstrated
5. Limitations, remaining gaps, and next steps

## Priority Summary

### Critical problems

1. The Portfolio does not yet function as an evidence-based doctoral portfolio.
2. The “Competency Evidence” section does not represent the program’s five official competency areas.
3. The Portfolio contains almost no actual artifacts, artifact descriptions, or evidence-specific reflections.
4. Doctoral residency was removed from the Portfolio even though the private source materials identify residency as a formal program component.
5. The integrative reflection is too brief and general to demonstrate doctoral development.
6. The homepage still promises “residency,” although the Portfolio no longer contains a residency section.

### High-priority problems

1. The coursework area resembles a transcript rather than a purposeful Program of Study.
2. Public display of every grade, including `W` and earlier lower grades, distracts from the portfolio’s academic argument.
3. Several research claims and role descriptions are not connected to publications, artifacts, or contribution statements.
4. Publication and conference status language is not consistently dated.
5. Teaching and professional appointments are presented as current accomplishments even when some begin in Fall 2026.
6. Tables lack several important accessibility features.
7. Small text is used extensively for labels, captions, navigation, metadata, and table headings.

### Medium-priority problems

1. Language and terminology are inconsistent across pages.
2. Several pages rely on attractive summaries without enough specific evidence.
3. Images lack loading and sizing optimizations.
4. Search-engine and social-sharing metadata are incomplete.
5. Keyboard focus treatment and mobile-menu behavior require stronger accessibility support.

# 1. Ph.D. Portfolio: Academic and Programmatic Problems

## 1.1 The Portfolio is not yet an evidence-based portfolio

### Problem

The page currently contains:

- A doctoral-journey diagram
- Course tables
- A short integrative reflection
- Four cards linking to other pages

This structure documents that activities exist, but it does not demonstrate what was learned or how doctoral competencies developed. A committee member cannot evaluate the quality of the work from links labeled Research, Scholarship, Teaching Practice, and Curriculum Vitae.

The four cards are navigation pathways, not competency evidence. A CV is a record of activity, not evidence of a specific competency.

### Required improvement

Replace the current card logic with an evidence structure for each official competency:

1. **Competency claim**
2. **Why the competency matters**
3. **Two or three selected artifacts**
4. **Context of each artifact**
5. **Yupei’s specific contribution**
6. **What the artifact demonstrates**
7. **Limitations or remaining development**
8. **Connection to dissertation preparation and future work**

Each artifact should link to a publication, paper excerpt, design document, analysis, presentation, approved image, or concise artifact page.

## 1.2 The official competency framework is missing

### Problem

The private competency planning document states that the SISLT portfolio must demonstrate development in five official areas. The website instead shows four broad pathways:

- Research and inquiry
- Scholarly communication
- Teaching and learning
- Professional record

These four categories do not preserve the program’s official five-area structure. “Professional record” is especially weak because it combines education, publications, leadership, service, and awards without identifying a competency.

### Required improvement

Reconstruct the section using the exact five competency names from the current SISLT handbook or an advisor-approved program document. Do not paraphrase the official categories until their wording has been verified.

Create a mapping table:

| Official competency | Selected evidence | Individual contribution | Reflection | Readiness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The public version can remain concise, but it must make the official framework visible.

## 1.3 Actual artifacts are absent

### Problem

The private coursework inventory recommends approximately six to eight carefully selected artifacts. None are currently presented as portfolio artifacts. The Research and Scholarship pages provide project summaries and citations, but they do not substitute for artifact-level portfolio evidence.

### Required improvement

Prioritize the following artifact types:

1. Research-design or literature-review artifact
2. Quantitative or measurement artifact
3. Qualitative-methods artifact
4. Action-research artifact
5. HCI, interaction-design, or front-end-analysis artifact
6. Learning-system or immersive-design artifact
7. Teaching or curriculum artifact
8. Professional-service or interdisciplinary-collaboration reflection

Every artifact page should answer:

- What was the task or problem?
- What did Yupei personally do?
- What knowledge or skill does the artifact demonstrate?
- What changed in Yupei’s thinking or practice?
- What limitation remains?
- How does this prepare Yupei for dissertation research?

## 1.4 Residency evidence is missing

### Problem

The homepage describes the Portfolio as including “coursework, competencies, residency, and reflection,” but residency was deleted from the Portfolio. The private residency document indicates that the Professional Immersion Residency Planning Form was approved and that residency activity spans research and writing, teaching, system development, service, and professional participation.

This creates both a factual inconsistency and a possible program-compliance gap.

### Required improvement

Confirm with the advisor whether residency must appear in the pre-comprehensive-examination portfolio. If required, restore a concise residency section containing:

- Official residency purpose
- Approved period or approval date
- Major activity categories
- Selected evidence
- Development across the residency
- Remaining residency goals

Do not publish the signed planning form. Present a public narrative and nonconfidential evidence.

If residency is not required on the public site, remove “residency” from the homepage pathway description.

## 1.5 The integrative reflection is insufficient

### Problem

The current reflection consists of one large quotation and one paragraph. It makes broad statements about connecting problems with evidence and interpreting multiple forms of data, but it does not identify:

- Specific turning points
- Concrete examples
- Difficulties or failed assumptions
- Changes in research identity
- Development of independence
- Connections among coursework, research, teaching, and service
- Preparation for comprehensive examinations
- Preparation for dissertation research
- Remaining gaps

The text is an introduction to a reflection, not an integrative doctoral reflection.

### Required improvement

Expand the reflection into approximately 1,200–1,800 words or a concise main-page summary linked to a full reflection. Organize it around development rather than chronology:

1. Transition from experienced educator to researcher
2. Development in research design and methods
3. Development as a learning-system designer
4. Growth in scholarly independence
5. Learning through interdisciplinary collaboration
6. Ethical and human-centered commitments
7. Current limitations
8. Dissertation direction and next steps

Use specific courses, projects, decisions, and artifacts as evidence.

## 1.6 The overview diagram lacks interpretive context

### Problem

The Overview now contains only the Learning → Designing → Researching → Contributing diagram. The diagram is understandable at a surface level, but it does not explain:

- Whether these are chronological stages or recurring practices
- How reflection influences each stage
- Where teaching, service, and collaboration belong
- How this model relates to program competencies

### Required improvement

Add a short two- or three-sentence introduction below the diagram. Explain that the stages are iterative and map them to the official competencies and selected evidence.

## 1.7 The Program of Study lacks an academic rationale

### Problem

The course tables show what was taken, but not why the courses form a coherent doctoral program. The supporting field is not named, the relationship between methods courses and the dissertation is not explained, and the site does not show how the course sequence supports the research agenda.

### Required improvement

Add a concise Program of Study rationale that explains:

- The supporting-field focus
- Why cognition, emotion, motivation, and resilience matter to the research agenda
- Why the selected qualitative, quantitative, measurement, and action-research courses were chosen
- How HCI, interaction design, games, VR, and web development support the learning-system research agenda
- Which courses directly prepare the planned dissertation

## 1.8 Coursework labels require revision

### Problems

- “Course Work” should normally be written as **“Coursework.”**
- “Master Degree Program Coursework” is grammatically weak. Use **“Master’s Degree Coursework”** or **“Coursework from the Master’s Degree Program.”**
- “The Other Courses During the Doctoral Period” is awkward and overly conversational.
- “15 hours of requirement coverage” is not immediately clear because only 12 hours appear in that table.
- “In progress” appears in the Grade column, although it is a status rather than a grade.
- `S` and `W` are not explained.

### Required improvement

Use the following titles:

- SISLT Doctoral Seminar Coursework
- Supporting-Field Coursework
- Research-Methods Coursework
- Additional Doctoral Coursework
- Master’s Degree Coursework

Add a short note or legend:

- `S` = Satisfactory
- `W` = Withdrawn
- `In progress` = Grade not yet assigned

Rewrite the methods summary as:

> 12 hours listed below, plus 3 hours from IS_LT 9411 shown under Doctoral Seminar Coursework.

## 1.9 Publishing every grade weakens the portfolio’s purpose

### Problem

The grade tables expose a full academic history, including a withdrawal and older lower grades. Unless the program explicitly requires public grades, this turns the Portfolio into a public transcript and shifts attention away from growth and evidence.

It also creates a long-term privacy and maintenance burden.

### Required improvement

Confirm whether grades are required by the program. If not:

- Remove the Grade column from the public website.
- Keep grades in the private Program of Study record.
- Report only completion status and credit hours publicly.

If grades must remain, add context explaining that the table is a self-reported Program of Study summary and not an official transcript.

## 1.10 The total-program picture is incomplete

### Problem

The page shows category totals but does not provide a clear summary of:

- Total unique completed doctoral hours
- Total in-progress hours
- Total SISLT hours
- Total methods hours
- Supporting-field hours
- Whether each minimum requirement has been met
- How repeated or cross-counted courses are handled

### Required improvement

Add a small, verified requirements summary. Avoid double counting. Clearly distinguish:

- Unique earned hours
- Requirement coverage
- In-progress hours
- Prior master’s coursework

Have the advisor verify this summary before publication.

# 8. Accessibility Problems

## 8.1 Coursework tables lack captions

### Problem

None of the five tables contains a `<caption>`. A screen-reader user must infer the table purpose from surrounding headings.

### Required improvement

Add a meaningful caption to each table, for example:

> SISLT doctoral seminar courses, terms, credit hours, and grades

Captions may be visually styled but should remain available to assistive technology.

## 8.2 Table header associations are incomplete

### Problem

The `<th>` cells do not use `scope="col"`. This weakens explicit association between headers and data cells.

### Required improvement

Add `scope="col"` to every column header. If future tables use row headings, use `scope="row"` appropriately.

## 8.3 Horizontally scrolling tables are not clearly discoverable

### Problem

On small screens, tables rely on `overflow-x: auto`, but users receive no visual or screen-reader instruction that horizontal scrolling is available. The scroll container is not keyboard focusable.

### Required improvement

- Add `tabindex="0"` to each table wrapper.
- Add an accessible label describing horizontal scrolling.
- Provide a subtle “Scroll horizontally to view all columns” cue on narrow screens.
- Consider converting tables to labeled cards on very small screens.

## 8.4 Text is frequently too small

### Problem

The stylesheet uses sizes such as `.58rem`, `.62rem`, `.64rem`, `.68rem`, `.7rem`, and `.72rem`. At a 16-pixel root size, these can render around 9–12 pixels. These sizes affect:

- Diagram labels
- Table headings
- Navigation metadata
- Project status labels
- Footer text
- Evidence labels
- Tags

This creates readability problems for low-vision users and on high-density mobile displays.

### Required improvement

- Use at least `0.875rem` for meaningful body metadata.
- Use at least `0.75rem` only for truly secondary labels.
- Avoid text smaller than 12 CSS pixels.
- Test at 200% zoom and with browser text enlargement.

## 8.5 Focus indication is inconsistent

### Problem

Some cards have focus styles, but many standard links and controls rely on browser defaults. The visual system emphasizes hover more consistently than keyboard focus.

### Required improvement

Create a global `:focus-visible` treatment with:

- A high-contrast outline
- Adequate offset
- No reliance on color alone

Test every navigation link, publication link, card, CV link, email link, and mobile-menu control by keyboard.

## 8.6 The mobile menu needs stronger focus management

### Problem

The menu updates `aria-expanded` and responds to Escape, but it does not:

- Move focus into the opened menu
- Keep keyboard focus within the menu when appropriate
- Return focus when the menu closes after outside interaction
- Update the screen-reader label from “Open navigation” to “Close navigation”

### Required improvement

Add state-aware accessible naming and tested focus behavior. At minimum:

- Change the button label when opened.
- Move focus to the first navigation link.
- Return focus to the button after closing with Escape.

## 8.7 Sticky navigation may obscure anchored headings

### Problem

The main header and Portfolio subnavigation are both sticky. Links to `#coursework`, `#reflection`, and `#evidence` may position content beneath the combined sticky bars.

### Required improvement

Add `scroll-margin-top` to anchored sections using a value large enough for both sticky bars.

## 8.8 Images lack performance attributes

### Problem

Most project images do not declare `width`, `height`, `loading`, or `decoding`. This can contribute to layout shifts and unnecessary initial downloads.

### Required improvement

- Add intrinsic width and height.
- Use `loading="lazy"` below the fold.
- Use `decoding="async"` for noncritical images.
- Compress images and provide modern WebP or AVIF alternatives.
- Keep the hero headshot optimized for its displayed size rather than loading a much larger source.

## 8.9 Diagram accessibility is only partially resolved

### Problem

The diagrams use `role="img"` and accessible labels, but the visible text and the accessible description may diverge as content changes. Some diagrams also use very small labels.

### Required improvement

- Keep visible and accessible descriptions synchronized.
- Provide a short text explanation immediately after each diagram.
- Do not rely on arrows, spatial arrangement, or color alone to communicate sequence.

## 8.10 Publication links are difficult to identify

### Problem

Arrow-only links provide limited visible affordance, and their repeated accessible label is nondistinctive.

### Required improvement

Use visible text such as “View publication” or a title-specific accessible name. Ensure the clickable target is at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels where practical.

# 10. Technical, Metadata, and Maintainability Problems

## 10.1 Social metadata is incomplete

### Problem

The homepage contains limited Open Graph metadata, but subpages do not have page-specific Open Graph titles, descriptions, images, or canonical URLs.

### Required improvement

Add to every public page:

- Canonical URL
- Open Graph title
- Open Graph description
- Open Graph URL
- Open Graph image
- Twitter/X card metadata

Use absolute URLs for social images.

## 10.2 Structured academic metadata is absent

### Problem

Search engines receive ordinary page text but no structured data for the person, publications, affiliation, or scholarly profiles.

### Required improvement

Add JSON-LD using appropriate Schema.org types:

- `Person`
- `EducationalOrganization`
- `ScholarlyArticle`
- `ProfilePage`

Verify all structured claims against public records.

## 10.3 Page-update dates are missing

### Problem

Research status, conference acceptance, appointments, service counts, grades, and in-progress coursework are time-sensitive.

### Required improvement

Add “Last updated” dates to:

- Portfolio
- Research
- Scholarship
- Teaching
- Engagement
- CV

## 10.4 Old and new site files coexist

### Problem

The `webdevelopment/` folder contains legacy pages as well as current assets. This makes maintenance confusing and increases the risk of editing or linking to an outdated file.

### Required improvement

Separate:

```text
assets/
legacy/
```

Move active images, fonts, icons, and documents into `assets/`. Keep redirects or archived files clearly labeled.

## 10.5 Stylesheet contains obsolete selectors

### Problem

CSS remains for removed sections such as `.privacy-note`, `.portfolio-principles`, `.competency-list`, and `.evidence-strip`. Obsolete styles make future revisions harder and can conceal unintended dependencies.

### Required improvement

Audit and remove selectors no longer used by any current page. Split the stylesheet into logical sections or files if continued growth makes one file difficult to maintain.

## 10.6 No automated accessibility or link test is part of publication

### Problem

Validation has checked HTML and local paths, but no repeatable WCAG audit, browser test, or external-link test is documented as a publication requirement.

### Required improvement

Add a prepublication checklist or automated workflow using:

- HTML validation
- axe or Lighthouse accessibility testing
- Broken-link checking
- Mobile viewport checks
- Performance checks

Do not treat a high automated score as a substitute for keyboard and screen-reader review.
