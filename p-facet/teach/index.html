The P-FACET Model · for instructors

Teaching with P-FACET

Generative AI redistributes cognitive work among learner, task, and machine. Your distinctive value is no longer holding information — it's defining which thinking must stay with the learner and making that thinking visible. This page holds the working tools: the offloading lens, six talk moves, an observation scaffold, and an implementation checklist.

The lens: four kinds of offloading

Judge AI use against the target cognitive work of your task — the same visible action can be fine in one assignment and corrosive in another.

KindWhat the tool doesExample
MechanicalPeripheral help; the target thinking is untouched.Formats citations; flags a syntax error.
SupportiveThinking support; the learner stays responsible.Critiques a learner draft against criteria.
SubstitutivePerforms the central work the learner was meant to practice.Writes the main argument before the learner tries.
RegulatedNot a fourth category — the metacognitive control over the other three.The learner explains why they revised AI output.
Two questions decide every case: first, what cognitive work did the tool perform — peripheral, supportive, or substitutive? The action doesn't classify itself; the objective does. Then, how did the learner govern the delegation — purpose set, boundary drawn, result evaluated, responsibility kept? Regulation is the learner's control over offloading, not a fourth kind of delegated work.

Six talk moves

Short questions that redirect over-delegation back to learner responsibility. Facilitation, not surveillance.

P"What problem are you addressing, and why does it matter?"
F"What are your goals, criteria, and constraints?"
A"What did you try or represent before AI?"
C"What specific contribution do you need from AI right now?"
E"What evidence supports that decision?"
T"What changed, and how will you use this strategy elsewhere?"

Observing evidence, not intentions

For each phase, record one concrete example and rate it: 0 substitutive · 1 emerging, partial · 2 clear and learner-owned · N/O not observed. Keep 0 and N/O distinct — a missing artifact is absent evidence, never proof of substitution.

Process evidence

  • framing notes
  • the human-first attempt
  • bounded AI prompts
  • the decision log
  • revision rationale
  • transfer reflection

Product evidence

  • accurate disciplinary content
  • coherent reasoning
  • meets task criteria
  • appropriate evidence
  • purposeful communication
  • a genuinely revised artifact
Assess both, ask both: what did the student produce — and what cognitive work did the student perform?

When you see it, say this

When you see…Try saying…
Writing: the student asks AI to draft the full essay."Draft your claim and evidence first; use AI to critique the logic."
Science: the student asks AI to interpret the graph."Name the pattern you see before asking AI to challenge it."
Math: the student asks AI for all the steps."Show your first strategy; ask AI for a hint or an error check."
Design: AI produces the final artifact."Define your criteria and compare alternatives before adopting anything."
A peripheral skill is blocking progress."Use AI freely for the technical part — keep the core decision yours."
The student accepts everything AI suggests."Pick one suggestion and corroborate it — what evidence backs it?"
The student rejects everything AI suggests."Is the skepticism evidence-based? Show me one check you ran."
The student can't evaluate the output at all."Let's build the criteria together — what would a good answer have to do?"
AI use is an accessibility or language support."The support stays, fully — let's just make sure the final judgment is still yours."

Redirect; don't punish. The goal is regulation, not detection.

Implementation checklist

Before Choose an authentic task · clarify the target cognitive work · set AI roles and boundaries · prepare framing and decision-log prompts · set privacy, attribution, and access rules During Watch the phases · ask the short questions above · collect process evidence · adapt scaffolding as competence grows After Assess process + product together · debrief the AI-use decisions · ask for a transfer reflection

Try it on one of your own assignments

Twenty-minute exercise: pick one activity — a case analysis, problem set, lab write-up, or essay — and answer three questions: What must students do before AI, with AI, and after AI? The Assignment Kit walks you through it and exports the result for your syllabus.

Assign to your students: the Quick Start (3 min) and Trainer (8 min) teach the routine; the Request Builder and Decision Log support it during real work; the Case Library shows regulated and substitutive paths side by side.
