The P-FACET Model · for instructors

Assignment Redesign Kit

Most AI policies list permitted tools. P-FACET asks a different design question: which cognitive work must stay with the learner? A P-FACET-aligned assignment makes five things explicit. Fill them in for one of your own assignments, then copy the block straight into your assignment sheet.

Everything you type stays in this browser — nothing is uploaded. Your draft saves automatically.

1

The authentic problem & its purpose

Anchor the task in a real problem worth caring about — not “use AI to complete X.”

2

The learner-generated representation required first

What must students produce before central work may be delegated? A frame, plan, sketch, prediction, or first draft — calibrated to their expertise.

3

The bounded roles AI may play

Name the permitted roles — critique, alternatives, questions, explanation, technical help — and any that are off-limits for this task.

4

The criteria & verification students judge output against

What counts as good — and how must AI output be checked before it is used? Verification proportional to risk: claims need sources; calculations need recomputation.

5

The evidence of transformation & transfer submitted

What proof of learner judgment accompanies the product? Keep it lightweight — visibility, not paperwork.

The failure test: before you ship the assignment, ask — could a learner satisfy every requirement above mechanically, without doing the intended reasoning? If yes, tighten elements 2 and 4.

Two completed examples

Position essay — campus food waste (writing-intensive course)
1 · Problem & purpose. Our campus discards an estimated 40 tons of food per semester. Write a feasible, evidence-based recommendation to dining services. The discipline’s work here: weighing evidence and making a defensible recommendation to a real audience.
2 · Required first representation. Before any AI use: a problem frame (goal, stakeholders, success criteria, assumptions) and a full first-draft recommendation built from course readings. Submitted with the final essay.
3 · Bounded AI roles (Standard designation). AI may critique your draft, generate counterarguments, or simulate a skeptical administrator. It may not draft or redraft any section. Every consultation goes in your Decision Log.
4 · Criteria & verification. Rubric: feasibility · evidence quality · counterargument handling · coherence. Any factual claim originating from AI must be verified against a citable source; uncited AI claims are treated as unsupported.
5 · Evidence submitted. Final essay + first draft + Decision Log + a three-sentence transfer note. The log is graded on quality of reasoning, not on which decisions were made.
Data interpretation lab — enzyme kinetics (STEM course)
1 · Problem & purpose. Given your team’s experimental data, determine whether the inhibitor is competitive or non-competitive, and justify the call. The target skill: reading evidence from a graph and defending an inference — not producing a polished paragraph.
2 · Required first representation. Before AI: annotate the plot by hand, name the pattern you see, and write your preliminary interpretation with its reasoning (three to five sentences, photo or scan accepted).
3 · Bounded AI roles (Standard designation). AI may challenge your interpretation, suggest alternative explanations for the pattern, or explain the underlying model. It may not interpret the graph for you. Peripheral help (formatting, spreadsheet syntax) is unrestricted.
4 · Criteria & verification. Correct mechanistic reasoning · use of the actual data · consideration of at least one alternative explanation. AI-suggested mechanisms must be checked against the textbook’s kinetics chapter before adoption.
5 · Evidence submitted. Annotated plot + preliminary interpretation + final report + Decision Log. A revised interpretation with a clear rationale earns full credit — changing your mind well is the skill.

Rubric rows for process evidence

Add alongside your product criteria. Mastery language: Meets · Developing · Not yet · No evidence. Score a missing artifact as No evidence — absence of a trace is not proof of substitution, and the two must never be conflated.
