When AI helps you, who is doing the thinking?

AI can draft, solve, analyze, and design. Used one way, it replaces your thinking; used another, it sharpens it. P-FACET is a six-phase routine for keeping the thinking that matters yours while still getting real help from AI. This page teaches you the routine by making you use it.

Part 2 · The core skill

Two questions decide every case

Handing work to a tool is called cognitive offloading — and it isn't automatically bad. Notes offload memory; calculators offload computation. Every scenario below is judged by two questions. What work was delegated? — Mechanical (peripheral), Supportive (thinking support), or Substitutive (the task's central work). And how was it governed? — regulated (a purpose, a boundary, an evaluated result the learner stays responsible for) or unregulated. Regulation is your control over the delegation — not a fourth kind of work.

The two questions that decide every case: first, judge the AI's action against the target cognitive work — the same action can be mechanical in one task and substitutive in another, depending on what the task was meant to teach you. Then judge the governance: did the learner set a purpose and a boundary, evaluate the result, and keep responsibility for the decision? Regulation is your control over the delegation — not a fourth kind of work.

Part 6 · Keep this

Your pocket card

Six questions to ask yourself, one per phase. If you can answer all six about a piece of work, your AI use was regulated — and your learning is visible.

PWhat problem am I solving, and why does it matter?
FWhat will count as a successful response? — goals, criteria, constraints, assumptions
AWhat did I try or create before consulting AI?
CWhat specific contribution do I need from AI right now? — critique, alternatives, explanation, technical help
EWhat criterion or evidence supports my decision to accept, revise, reject, remix, or defer?
TWhat changed, and how will I use this strategy elsewhere?

AI should support

  • alternatives
  • critique
  • explanation
  • technical help
  • reflection

AI should not replace

  • problem framing
  • core reasoning
  • judgment
  • authorship
  • revision & transfer
