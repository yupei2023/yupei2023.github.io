How to use P-FACET when you work with AI

Six moves, in a cycle. Green moves are yours — that's where the learning happens. Blue is the one move where you talk to AI, and even there, you set the boundary. Loop back whenever you need to.

PFACET
P

Start from the problem

You

Name the problem you're solving, why it matters, and what you personally want to learn from it.

No AI yet — the purpose stays yours.
Ask yourself: "What problem am I solving, and why does it matter?"
F

Frame it

You

Write down your goal, who it's for, what success looks like (criteria), your constraints, and your assumptions.

AI may ask you questions or poke at your assumptions — it never writes the frame for you.
Ask yourself: "What will count as a successful response?"
A

Attempt it first

You

Recall what you know, think of similar problems, and make a first draft, sketch, solution, or plan. It doesn't need to be good — it needs to exist.

AI shows examples only after your attempt.
Ask yourself: "What did I try before consulting AI?"
C

Consult — with a boundary

You + AI

Find the one specific gap in your work, then make a bounded request: critique, alternatives, an explanation, or technical help. Never hand over the whole task.

C is a gateway, not a single moment — whenever a new gap appears in F, E, or T, you pass through here again, then bring the answer back to your own judgment.
Prompt starters that keep the thinking yours
"Here's my draft. Critique the logic against my criteria — don't rewrite it."
"Here's my interpretation. Challenge it — what other explanations fit?"
"Here's my setup and first steps. Don't solve it — flag any error and give one hint."
"Here are my criteria and two options. Compare them — what trade-offs am I missing?"
Ask yourself: "What specific contribution do I need from AI right now?"
E

Evaluate and explain

You

Check AI's output against your criteria and real evidence. Then decide — accept, revise, reject, remix, or defer — and write down why. "I checked it" doesn't count; naming your evidence does.

AI can offer counterarguments — the verdict is yours.
Ask yourself: "What criterion or evidence supports my decision?" — and if evaluating exposes a gap, loop back to F, A, or C. That's the cycle working.
T

Transform and transfer

You

Fold your decisions into work you own, note what changed and why, and name where you'll use this strategy next time.

AI can help you compare contexts — where else would this work?
Ask yourself: "What changed, and how will I use this strategy elsewhere?"
Use it right now

Walk a real task through the cycle

Open the assignment you're working on today. Tick each box only when you can honestly answer the question — that's the whole method.

0 of 6
All six — your AI use was regulated, and the thinking on record is yours. That's P-FACET.
