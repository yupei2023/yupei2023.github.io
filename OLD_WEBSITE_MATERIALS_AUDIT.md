## Materials that should not be migrated unchanged

- Posts that identify former students or reproduce their work should be anonymized and reviewed for consent.
- Legacy downloads should be scanned for personal information, copyright restrictions, and accessibility before publication.
- Old CNTV/CCTV, school, Canvas, and external-resource links should be tested; several platforms and URL structures may have changed.
- Draft course reflections should be professionally edited and reframed with current hindsight rather than copied verbatim.
- The 95 popular-science posts and 71 question-and-answer posts should remain an archive, with only a representative selection incorporated into the main academic website.
- Claims about publication, translation, awards, audience numbers, or institutional roles should be checked against the CV, publisher records, certificates, or other primary evidence.

## Overall assessment

The old website’s greatest value is not additional biography. Its strongest contribution is evidence of continuity: long-term science communication, classroom inquiry, curriculum leadership, early online and VR teaching, and a documented transition into game-based and immersive-learning design. The current website already communicates the present research identity effectively; carefully selected legacy case studies would make the professional journey and the foundations of that research identity substantially more convincing.
